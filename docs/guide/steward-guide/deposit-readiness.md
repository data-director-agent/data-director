# Deposit readiness

The weeks before a deposit are often when a researcher most needs you. This is when the
Blueprint's checks fall due: ethics approval, personal data, third-party material, metadata
quality and the repository's own requirements.

## What the researcher goes through

On the project's **Deposit** tab the researcher:

1. chooses a repository, and validates the project against its requirements;
2. decides each file the check flags (field notes, coordinates, someone else's material, an
   agreement, a possible identifier): keep it, leave it out, or change it;
3. confirms ethics approval, data protection advice and third-party rights;
4. builds the package and deposits it.

See [Depositing your data](../user-guide/depositing.md). Steps 1 to 4 work today. The researcher
makes every decision.

With access to the project, you see the deposit tab under the steward banner, read-only. You can:

- **add a note** to a flagged item or a failed validation rule, which the researcher sees beside
  it with your name;
- **propose an edit** to a document that needs fixing ([Working in a project](working-in-a-project.md));
- **pause** the project if the deposit must not go ahead until something is checked.

## Co-approval

If your profile sets `stewardship.deposit_co_approval: true`, a deposit also needs a steward's
co-approval. Once the researcher approves the package, the tab shows that it is waiting for you.
You either **co-approve** the package, naming its digest and giving a reason, or you **ask for
changes**. A new package needs a new co-approval.

Co-approval is an institutional check (§5.1). It never replaces the researcher's approval, and
the researcher still makes the deposit. You cannot co-approve a project you are a member of.
