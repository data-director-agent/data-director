# Good practice

Researchers will trust stewards who use Data Director well. The rules below are built into the
app, and these habits help as well.

- **Look only when you need to.** Every look is shown to the researcher. Open a project because a
  matter needs you, not out of curiosity. Use the [community overview](overviews.md) for the
  wider picture.
- **Say why.** Clearing or refusing a step, pausing, resolving and handing back all ask for a
  reason, and the researcher reads it. Write it for them, in plain words.
- **Propose rather than fix.** The work and its approval are the researcher's (R2, §8). Propose an
  edit, explain it, and let them decide. The record will show that you helped, credited to you as
  *Data curation* or *Validation*.
- **Hand back what is theirs.** Handing back is not failing. When the next step is the
  researcher's, say so and say what it is.
- **Pause sparingly, and lift promptly.** A pause stops a person's work. Use it when something must
  not go further, say what you are checking, and lift it as soon as you can.
- **Mind the personal data.** An escalation should hold what is needed and nothing more. Never copy
  research content into notifications, tickets or email.
- **Keep your own expertise.** The agents are advisers. Check what they say against what you know,
  and tell the project when they are wrong ([Giving feedback](../user-guide/feedback.md)). The
  Blueprint warns against over-reliance eroding in-house expertise (§5.5).
- **Declare a conflict.** If you work on a project yourself, act there as a member, and let a
  colleague handle its escalations.
