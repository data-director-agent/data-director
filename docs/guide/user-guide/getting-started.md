# Getting started

This page takes you from opening the app to your first useful answer. It needs no technical
knowledge and about ten minutes. Everything it mentions is explained in more detail later in the
guide.

## Before you begin

!!! warning "Use test data only"
    Data Director is an alpha prototype. Describe a made-up project, or one whose details are
    already public, while you try it. Do not enter personal data, unpublished results or anything
    confidential. The short [alpha usage agreement](usage-agreement.md) explains why, and by using
    the app you agree to it.

You need:

- **The address of a Data Director.** Your institution, or whoever is running a workshop or trial
  you are part of, gives you this. Data Director is not a service you sign up to on the public
  web: each one is run by an organisation that chooses which advisers it offers and where your
  information is kept. If you are not sure whether your institution has one, ask your library or
  research data support team.
- **A web browser.** Nothing needs installing on your computer for the conversation itself.
- **An ORCID iD**, if the instance asks you to sign in. ORCID is the free, standard identifier
  for researchers ([get one at orcid.org](https://orcid.org/register)). Data Director asks ORCID
  only for your iD and your name.
- **A rough idea of your project**: what it is about, what funder or institution supports it, and
  what kind of data you have or expect to have. A proposal or award letter is helpful, but not
  required.

Running Data Director on your own computer is possible but is meant for developers and trial
runs: see [Setting up](../developer-guide/setup.md).

## 1. Open the app and sign in

Open the address you were given. If the instance asks you to sign in, choose **Sign in with ORCID**
and approve the request on ORCID's own page. You are brought back to the Data Director, and your
name appears in the banner at the top.

Where nobody signs in, the banner says so. Every run is then shared with everyone using that
instance, which is another reason to use test data only. How your sign-in is used is in
[What the Data Director records](records.md#who-can-see-the-records).

## 2. Say hello

The home page is a conversation. There is a message box at the bottom. You do not need to use any
special words or commands: write as you would to a helpful colleague in research support.

You could start with something like:

> I'm a PhD student starting a project on river water quality. I'll collect sensor readings and
> some interview recordings. Where should I begin with my data?

If you would rather not type, an empty conversation offers **Suggested ways to start**. Choosing
one puts a message in the box for you to change and send. Nothing is sent until you send it.

## 3. Answer a few setup questions

When you start a new project, the Data Director first asks a few short questions to find out where
you are: just beginning, with data already collected, or ready to deposit. These take the message
box's place, and you can:

- pick from the choices, or select **Reply in my own words instead** to type an answer;
- skip any question you cannot answer yet;
- attach a document, such as your proposal, with **Attach a file**.

When you have answered, the Data Director tells you which stage of the
[workflow](workflow.md) fits you and suggests a first step. Select **Start with** that step to begin
there, or **Not now** and write your own message.

## 4. Read what comes back

A reply from the Data Director can contain a few kinds of thing:

- **Advice and explanations**, in plain language.
- **Questions** it needs answered, which you can answer in the conversation or leave for later.
- **Cards from specialists.** When it asks an agent for help, the agent's work appears as a card.
  Open one to see what the agent was given, what it found and the sources it used.
- **Proposals.** When an agent works something out, such as your funder, a repository your funder
  requires or what data you will collect, it is kept as a *proposal*. Nothing becomes a decision
  until you confirm it ([Deciding project facts](project-facts.md)).
- **Drafts.** A README, data dictionary or data management plan appears as a document you can
  read, edit, answer questions on and approve.

Three things are worth knowing from the start:

- **It advises and you decide.** Agents never choose your repository, licence or access level.
- **It says when it cannot help.** Rather than guess, an agent *abstains* and tells you why. You
  can then [ask a data steward](ask-a-data-steward.md). See [Understanding outcomes](outcomes.md).
- **Check its advice.** It is AI, and it can be wrong. Its cards show their sources so that you
  can check them.

## 5. Carry on

You can stop at any time. Your conversation and project are stored, and you reopen them from
**Projects** in the menu at the top. From here, pick whatever matches what you need:

| I want to… | Go to |
|---|---|
| Know where I am in the research data lifecycle | [The workflow](workflow.md) |
| Know who the specialists are and what they will and will not do | [The agents](agents.md) |
| Get help writing a README or metadata record for data I already have | [Making an inventory](making-an-inventory.md) |
| Let the Data Director read my proposal or my institution's policy | [Attaching a document](attaching-a-document.md) |
| Add a colleague to my project | [Working on a project with others](members.md) |
| Speak to a person | [Asking a data steward](ask-a-data-steward.md) |
| Get my data ready to deposit | [Depositing your data](depositing.md) |

## If something goes wrong

- **A reply fails.** The app says so in plain words and keeps anything you attached. Select
  **Try again** under the message.
- **You are not sure what a reply means.** See [Understanding outcomes](outcomes.md).
- **The advice looks wrong.** Please tell the project, which is how it improves:
  [Giving feedback](feedback.md), or **Report a problem** in the app.
- **You think you entered real data by mistake.** Tell the people running your instance straight
  away so that it can be removed.
